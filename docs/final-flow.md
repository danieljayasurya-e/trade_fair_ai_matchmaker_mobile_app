# Team 4 – Trade Fair AI Matchmaker: Final Flow

## Goal and core idea

We build a two-way, explainable matchmaker. A guest fills a short form and gets the top 30 exhibitors in under 2 seconds, each with a match % and a "Matched on" reason. Exhibitors see their best-fit guests as anonymised leads, and both sides update live when a guest edits their profile.

Our edge over other teams is **mutual fit**. The score combines two checks:

- **Guest → Exhibitor:** does this stall sell what the guest wants to buy?
- **Exhibitor → Guest:** does this guest match the exhibitor's target buyer (industry, seniority, company size)?

Final score = geometric mean of the two, normalised to 0–100%. A stall ranks high only when the fit works both ways.

What we avoid: a plain "send profile to an LLM and ask it to rank" wrapper. The rubric scores that as Level 2 ("superficial wrappers").

## Data setup (done before the demo)

All data is synthetic and generated by one script. It must be ready by Friday morning.

| Dataset | Size | What it holds | Why we need it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Exhibitors | ~200 | Name, products, industry verticals, target buyer criteria, hall/booth, messy free-text description | The list every guest is matched against |
| Seeded guests | ~500 | Industry, job role, seniority, company size, region, procurement interests, free text, opt-in flag | Feeds the exhibitor portal leads and the fairness view; new form users join this pool |
| Taxonomy | ~40–60 tags | Shared industry and product categories (e.g. Industrial Automation, Component Sourcing) | Makes "Matched on" reasons consistent on both sides |

Steps:

1. Generate exhibitors and guests with an LLM script, then save as JSON and load into Postgres.
2. Run an LLM pass once to extract taxonomy tags from each free-text description. Cache the results.
3. Embed every exhibitor and guest profile with a local model (e.g. all-MiniLM) and store vectors in pgvector.

Nothing here calls the internet during the demo.

## Matching engine pipeline

All slow AI work happens once, offline. A live request only embeds one guest, searches and re-ranks, which keeps it well under 2 seconds.

Pipeline: OFFLINE (before demo): exhibitor + guest data → LLM tagging (once, cached) → local embeddings (all-MiniLM) → Postgres + pgvector index.
LIVE (per guest request): guest form or sample → embed guest (local model) → vector search top 100 against exhibitor vectors → re-rank with two-way score → top 30 + "Matched on" reasons.

The re-rank step scores each of the 100 candidates on four factors:

- **Semantic similarity:** guest vs exhibitor embeddings.
- **Interest overlap:** shared taxonomy tags. These become the "Matched on" chips.
- **Intent match:** procurement interests vs the exhibitor's products.
- **Buyer fit:** the guest vs the exhibitor's target buyer criteria.

The first three form the guest → exhibitor score; buyer fit forms the exhibitor → guest score. The final match % is their geometric mean. Each factor's value is returned so the UI can show the breakdown bars.

## Guest flow (web and mobile)

The web app and the mobile app follow the same flow and call the same API.

1. **Landing page:** a short hook ("200 stalls. Find your 30 in 2 seconds.") and a "Find my stalls" button.
2. **Details form:** industry, job role, company size, procurement interests, and a free-text "what I'm looking for". Add 2–3 **"Try a sample profile"** buttons that fill the form instantly for the demo.
3. **Loading state:** a clear indicator while the engine runs.
4. **Top 30 results:** each card shows exhibitor name, match %, hall/booth, product categories and "Matched on" chips. A latency badge shows the time taken (e.g. "Top 30 in 180 ms").
5. **Search and filter:** by score range, industry, hall and product category; sort by score, hall or name.
6. **Stall detail:** opens on tap. Shows the score breakdown bars (semantic, interests, buyer fit, intent) and the full rationale.
7. **Bookmarks:** save stalls to "My visit list".
8. **Plan my route:** orders bookmarked stalls by hall and booth to cut walking.
9. **Download PDF:** exports the visit list with booth details and reasons.
10. **Edit my details:** the list recomputes and cards animate to their new ranks.

Bookmarks and profile edits sync between web and mobile because both use the same backend.

## Exhibitor portal (stretch goal)

The portal reuses the same scores, read from the exhibitor's side. Build it only after the guest flow works end to end.

1. **Exhibitor picker / login.**
2. **Dashboard:** estimated footfall, lead count, and charts for seniority, purchase intent, region and company size.
3. **Lead list:** a selector for top 20, 50 or 100 guests. Rows show match %, role level and industry. Names and contacts appear only for guests who opted in.
4. **Live updates:** when a guest edits their profile on mobile, the leads and charts refresh through Socket.io.

## Trust layer: fairness, privacy, fallbacks

This section earns the Responsible AI points and protects the live demo.

- **No boosts:** no sponsor, booth-size or paid ranking. Scores come only from profile fit.
- **Fairness view:** a small admin page showing how many exhibitors appear in at least one guest's top 30, and how evenly exposure is spread.
- **Privacy:** the exhibitor portal shows aggregates only. Any group with fewer than 5 guests is hidden. Contact details appear only for opted-in guests.
- **Offline fallback:** the local embedding model plus cached LLM tags mean the demo runs without internet.
- **Visible latency:** a loading indicator and the latency badge, as the brief asks.

## Live demo script and rubric mapping

The live demo slot is 3:30–6:30 of our 10 minutes. Run it in this order:

1. Open the landing page and click a sample profile. The top 30 appear with the latency badge.
2. Filter by hall or industry. Open the #1 stall and walk through its score breakdown and "Matched on" reasons.
3. Bookmark three stalls, tap "Plan my route" and download the PDF.
4. On mobile, edit the guest's interests. The guest list and the exhibitor portal update live.
5. Close on the fairness view.
